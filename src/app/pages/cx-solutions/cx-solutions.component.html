03 The problem

Most “AI for CX” is a better answer to a question nobody asked.

Chatbots retrieve articles. Copilots draft replies. Both leave the actual work - issuing the refund, changing the policy, rebooking the flight - sitting in a queue with your team's name on it. The customer still waits. Cost per contact barely moves.

An agent differs in one specific way: it is allowed to act. Given a goal, a set of systems and a hard boundary, a DIOnce agent reasons through the steps, calls the tools it needs, finishes the task, and escalates to a human only where judgement genuinely belongs.

That difference is the entire product. Everything below exists to make it safe enough to switch on.

06 Knowledge base & knowledge graph

It knows your policies. Not the internet.

One question - “can I return this?” - resolved across five linked records and a clause, in a single turn.

The agent is grounded in one thing: your own documented truth. Internal policies, standard operating procedures and FAQs go in as written - no rewriting, no summarising, no retraining.

On top of that sits a knowledge graph. Documents alone can only answer what a single page says. A graph knows that this customer holds that plan, which is governed by this refund clause, which has an exception for orders placed during a sale - and can answer the question that spans all four.

What goes in
Internal policy documents, SOPs, FAQs, help-centre articles, product and pricing tables, and past resolved tickets. Synced from Confluence, SharePoint, Notion, Google Drive and your help centre, so the agent updates when your documentation does.
How it answers
Retrieval is grounded and cited. Every response carries the source document and clause behind it, visible in the trace - so a disputed answer is a two-minute check, not an investigation.
Versions & dates
Policies carry effective dates. A query about an order placed in March is answered against March's policy, not today's. Superseded versions stay available for audit.
Scope & access
Knowledge is scoped per agent, per market and per channel. An internal-facing agent can see what a customer-facing one cannot.
Gaps, closed
When the agent can't find an answer, that gap is logged as a theme rather than a one-off. Recurring gaps get drafted into new articles based on how your care team actually resolved them - you review and approve.
No answer is a valid answer
If the knowledge base doesn't cover it, the agent says so and hands over. It will not fill the silence with something plausible.

09 Live call handling

Interruption isn't an edge case. It's how people talk.

Real calls are messy. People talk over you, change their mind halfway, and sometimes need a person right now. Two different things can interrupt a live call, and they're handled differently.

When the caller interrupts

Barge-in is treated as normal conversation, not an error state. The agent stops talking, listens, and picks up from where the caller took it - without restarting its explanation from the top.

  • Stops mid-word. Speech halts within ~200ms of detecting the caller's voice, the way a person would.
  • Keeps the thread. The half-finished turn stays in context, so “no, the other order” resolves correctly instead of starting the flow again.
  • Knows a person from a television. Voice activity detection plus speaker separation means background noise, a passing conversation or a crying child doesn't trigger a false interruption.
  • Handles corrections. “Actually, make it the blue one” updates the in-flight action rather than queuing a second one.
  • Tolerates silence. Thinking pauses aren't filled with a prompt after two seconds. It waits, the way a good rep does.

When a human steps in

Your supervisors can watch, coach or take over any live call - mid-sentence if they need to - without the caller repeating anything.

  • Live monitoring. Any call in progress can be listened to, with the running trace visible beside it.
  • Whisper mode. A supervisor can coach the agent's next turn without the caller hearing.
  • One-click takeover. A human takes the call from the current sentence. The full transcript, customer record and everything the agent has already done arrives with it.
  • Automatic handover triggers. Repeated failure, falling sentiment, a request for a person, or any topic you've placed off-limits routes to your team before the caller has to insist.
  • Hand back cleanly. Once the human is done, the agent can resume for the routine remainder - confirmations, follow-up scheduling, the wrap-up note.
  • Nothing is lost. Both halves of the call sit in one record, one transcript, one audit trail.

11 Reliability engineering

The unglamorous part that decides whether this works.

A language model on its own is a probability distribution with a customer attached to it. Here is the machinery that makes it dependable enough to answer your phone.

Supervisory layer

Every response passes a checking layer before it reaches a customer - grounding, policy, tone and abuse detection. Invented answers get caught in the system, not in the transcript.

Multi-model by design

Reasoning, comprehension and generation route to whichever model performs best for your workload, mixing frontier and open-weight. New models get swapped in as they improve, without you rebuilding anything.

Provider failover

If a model provider degrades, traffic moves automatically. Your contact centre does not have one vendor's status page as a single point of failure.

Escalation as a feature

Confidence thresholds, sentiment triggers, explicit customer requests and any topic you've ruled out all route to a person with full context attached. Handing off well is a design goal, not an admission of defeat.

Deterministic where it matters

Money movement, identity and anything irreversible run through fixed, audited paths. The agent chooses to act; the action itself is code.

Grounded, or silent

If the answer isn't in your knowledge base, the agent doesn't compose one. It says so, and routes the caller to someone who can help.

13 Company policy & regulatory compliance

Your policy is the specification.

Security certifications say your data is safe. They say nothing about whether the agent followed your rules on the call. This is that layer - your company's own policies, and the regulations you answer to, enforced turn by turn.

Policy as configuration
Your actual policy documents define what the agent may do. Refund ceilings, approval thresholds, eligibility rules and escalation triggers are derived from them and versioned alongside them - not re-typed into a settings page where the two can drift apart.
Mandatory disclosures
Call-recording consent, regulatory disclaimers, terms read-outs and identity verification are spoken every time, in the caller's language, and logged as having been delivered.
Prohibited claims
No guarantees of outcome, no advice beyond the agent's scope, no commitments outside its authority. Blocked at the response layer, before the caller hears it.
Contact & consent rules
DND and do-not-call registries, permitted calling windows, frequency caps and opt-outs honoured within the turn they're requested. Consent captured, timestamped and retrievable.
Per-market policy sets
One agent, different rules by geography, legal entity or product line. A caller in the UAE and a caller in Karnataka get the same service and different obligations, correctly applied.
Change control
Policy edits go through review and approval before they reach a live agent. Every version dated, every change attributed, agents pinnable to an effective date.
Evidence on demand
For any conversation, months later: which policy version was in force, which clause drove the decision, what the agent was and wasn't permitted to do at that moment. Built for the complaint and the audit, not just the dashboard.

15 Questions everyone asks

The awkward ones, answered.

Is “under an hour” real?

For a first working agent on a defined use case with an accessible system to connect to - yes, and we'll do it live on a call. A full production rollout, including your security review and traffic ramp, typically runs three to six weeks.

How is this different from the AI bundled with our contact centre platform?

Bundled AI answers inside a conversation. It rarely acts across your other systems, it's rarely grounded in your actual policy documents, and it almost never gives you turn-level observability. Without those you can't prove the agent is behaving - which is why most bundled deployments stall at FAQ deflection and never reach the work that costs you money.

What about Sierra, Decagon and Ada?

Strong platforms with enterprise motions: long sales cycles, guided onboarding measured in weeks, no published pricing, US-hosted. If you're a Fortune 100 with a year and a steering committee, they're a serious option. If you want to be live this quarter, in your own cloud, in Indian languages, at a price you can see before you talk to anyone - that's us.

What happens when the agent doesn't know?

It escalates, with full context attached. Confidently guessing is the failure mode we optimise hardest against, and you set the threshold yourself.

Do we need to replace our contact centre?

No. DIOnce sits on top of what you already run - telephony, CRM and helpdesk stay exactly where they are.

Who owns the data?

You do. Always. Exportable at any time, including your agent definitions, so leaving is a decision rather than a project.

Can we run it in our own cloud?

Yes - AWS, Azure, GCP or on-premise, with data residency in India, Singapore, the EU, the US or the UAE.
